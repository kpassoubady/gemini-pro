# Session 5 Research: Persistent Personas

**Research date:** 2026-08-26
**Course:** Gemini Pro
**Session:** Day 1, Session 5 — Persistent Personas

## Scope and current product behavior

Gems are saved, customized versions of Gemini for repeatable tasks. Current Gemini Apps help documentation says custom Gems are created in the Gemini web app by naming the Gem and writing instructions; users can optionally add knowledge files. A Gem can then be used in Gemini web and mobile experiences, and a web-created Gem may appear in the Workspace side panel. Availability, account terms, and feature details can vary, so the instructor should verify the current interface before class.

Google’s guidance recommends covering some combination of persona, task, context, and format. For the course’s Relentless Editor, the instructions should define a coaching role, ask it to identify clichés and logical gaps, require questions rather than an automatic rewrite, and specify a direct tone. Learners should preview and test the Gem, then click Save; previewing alone does not automatically save it.

## Canvas workflow

Canvas provides a side-by-side space for creating or editing documents with Gemini. Current help documentation describes selecting text and asking for a targeted edit, changing length or tone, making direct edits, and navigating saved versions. The session should teach selective refinement: move a draft into Canvas, highlight one paragraph, ask for a specific change, inspect the suggestion, and keep or reject it. The learner remains the editor and must preserve the intended meaning and voice.

## Practical design pattern

Write Gem instructions as a small contract: role, recurring task, context, response format, boundaries, and a question to ask when context is missing. Test with two drafts that expose different failure modes, such as cliché-heavy prose and a logically incomplete argument. Record one instruction change and the observed difference. Do not include confidential drafts or proprietary policy text unless the account and organization explicitly permit it.

## Industry relevance

Questrade reports using Gems to save writing time, while Beyond describes using Gemini to draft answers to repetitive RFI questions from current Drive content. These customer stories illustrate repeatable drafting and knowledge-grounded assistance, but they are reported outcomes, not guaranteed results. The lesson should emphasize a reusable workflow with review rather than autonomous publishing.

## Risks and guardrails

A persistent instruction can preserve a bad assumption across many tasks. Gems may produce confident edits, flatten a writer’s voice, or use uploaded knowledge outside the intended context. Review the first outputs, avoid sensitive source material, and keep a human approval step before external communication. Canvas auto-saves changes, so learners should use version navigation and retain a clean original when experimenting.
