# Session 18 Research: Gemini Notebook Capstone and Workflow Integration

**Research date:** 2026-08-27
**Course:** Gemini Pro
**Session:** Day 2, Session 18, Gemini Notebook: Capstone Project and Workflow Integration

## Capstone purpose

The capstone should test the complete evidence-to-artifact workflow rather than reward the largest notebook or most polished generated output. Each learner selects a fresh, non-sensitive work or study topic, creates a notebook, imports and inspects sources, asks a cross-source question, verifies claims, saves analytical notes, creates a study guide, and produces one reviewed multimedia artifact.

The final evidence should show how the learner controlled sources and corrected output. Require a source register, one claim-evidence matrix, one saved verified note, one unresolved question, one study guide, one multimedia artifact, and one sharing or retention decision.

## End-to-end operating model

A practical Gemini Notebook workflow follows these stages:

1. **Define:** State the audience, decision, deliverable, owner, and data boundary.
2. **Source:** Add only permitted material and record authority, freshness, scope, and origin.
3. **Inspect:** Compare imported content with originals and check synchronization status.
4. **Interrogate:** Ask a bounded cross-source question with an evidence format.
5. **Verify:** Open citations, preserve conflict, and mark unsupported claims.
6. **Curate:** Save verified notes and label any derived source.
7. **Transform:** Generate a study guide and one fit-for-purpose multimedia artifact.
8. **Review:** Check facts, accessibility, rights, brand, and source fidelity.
9. **Share:** Apply notebook, artifact, and exported-file permissions separately.
10. **Maintain:** Assign an owner, refresh date, review trigger, and archive or deletion rule.

The last stage matters because synchronized source documents can change while exported Docs, Sheets, downloads, and previously approved decisions remain static. A useful notebook needs a maintenance contract, not only a creation checklist.

## Integration across Gemini and Workspace

Notebook names, sources, and custom instructions sync between Gemini Notebook and notebooks in Gemini Apps. Standard notebook chat supports controlled source work, while eligible agentic features or Gemini Apps can add tools, web evidence, calculations, and files. Users should label which surface and mode produced each output.

Use product handoffs deliberately:

| Need | Surface or handoff |
| :--- | :--- |
| Controlled source synthesis | Gemini Notebook chat |
| Web research, code, or broader tools | Eligible agentic chat or Gemini Apps |
| Persistent verified analysis | Notebook notes |
| Structured report | Studio report, then reviewed export to Docs |
| Data table | Studio table, then reviewed export to Sheets |
| Editable presentation | Google Slides workflow after evidence approval |
| Collaborative video production | Google Vids after storyboard and script approval |

Exports are copies with separate permissions and do not synchronize changes back to the notebook. A Gemini Notebook artifact can support a later Slides or Vids workflow, but the receiving team must preserve citations and recheck claims after editing.

## Ownership, sharing, and access

Private notebooks support Viewer and Editor roles. Viewers can read shared sources and notes. Editors can add or remove sources and notes and may share the notebook further. Give Editor only to people responsible for maintaining the evidence set.

Chat View provides a focused experience but does not reliably revoke underlying access to sources and artifacts. Users may still navigate to hidden notebook content. Do not use Chat View as a security boundary. Remove restricted sources or create a separate audience-safe notebook instead.

Public notebook sharing is available for consumer accounts but disabled for Workspace Enterprise and Education accounts. Workspace learners should use organization-approved sharing. Downloaded audio, video, image, and exported Workspace files leave the notebook permission model and need their own access, retention, and approval controls.

## Maintenance and quality controls

Assign these fields before handoff:

- Notebook owner and backup owner.
- Source owners and synchronization status.
- Last verification date and next review date.
- Trigger events such as policy change, new report, expired data, or team reorganization.
- Approved viewers and editors.
- Exported artifact inventory and destination permissions.
- Archive, deletion, and incident-response rules.

Notebook analytics can show users and queries per day for qualifying shared notebooks with enough users and recent activity. Analytics indicates adoption, not answer quality. Pair usage data with sampled citation reviews, reported defects, and source freshness checks.

## Capstone topic choices

Offer learners a fresh topic that fits one of these authentic patterns:

1. **Employee onboarding:** Policies, role guides, frequently asked questions, and a manager Audio Overview.
2. **Product launch readiness:** Tracker, announcement, requirements, risk notes, and an infographic.
3. **Client or market briefing:** Approved internal material plus dated public research, with evidence boundaries labeled.
4. **Professional development:** Course readings, notes, and a verified study guide with quiz.

Every topic must use non-sensitive material or a prepared fictional pack. Learners should not import customer records, confidential strategy, protected personal data, or copyrighted material they cannot use.

## Acceptance rubric

Score the capstone on observable evidence:

| Criterion | Passing evidence |
| :--- | :--- |
| Source quality | At least three diverse sources classified for authority and freshness |
| Grounding | One cross-source answer with opened and checked citations |
| Judgment | One corrected, rejected, partial, or unresolved claim documented |
| Notes | At least one verified note retaining source traceability |
| Study aid | One study guide checked against primary sources |
| Multimedia | One audio or visual artifact reviewed for facts and accessibility |
| Governance | Notebook owner, audience, permissions, and maintenance trigger recorded |

Do not score visual polish above source quality or verification. A simple artifact with a clear defect log should outperform an impressive output with unchecked claims.

## Facilitation plan

This session does not need a separate instructor demo because Sessions 13, 15, and 17 already demonstrated the mechanisms. Use a brief capstone launch, independent build time, a midpoint evidence checkpoint, a final peer review, volunteer showcases, and question-and-answer time.

A practical 60-minute flow is:

- 0-5: Choose topic, audience, and deliverable.
- 5-15: Create notebook, add sources, and complete the source register.
- 15-27: Ask the cross-source question and verify citations.
- 27-37: Save notes and generate the study guide.
- 37-47: Create and review one multimedia artifact.
- 47-54: Complete governance and maintenance checks.
- 54-58: Peer review against the acceptance rubric.
- 58-60: Volunteer evidence share and final reflection.

## Recommended companion exercise

Breakout Lab 2.6 should provide a `start/` capstone record, an acceptance rubric, and a small fictional fallback source pack. The `solution/` should demonstrate a different topic from the policy notebook used in Labs 2.3 to 2.5 so learners see transfer rather than repetition. A product-launch or employee-onboarding example fits the industry research and uses the same evidence controls.

## Common failure modes

- Reusing the earlier policy notebook instead of demonstrating transfer to a fresh topic.
- Spending most of the capstone generating media before checking source authority.
- Choosing all sources for every question.
- Saving or exporting unsupported synthesis without a defect record.
- Granting Editor access to a broad audience.
- Treating Chat View as a security control that hides underlying sources.
- Assuming exported Docs, Sheets, audio, video, or images remain synchronized.
- Ending without an owner, review trigger, or archive decision.
