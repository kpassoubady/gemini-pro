# Gemini Pro (6 Sessions × 1 Hour, Delivered Across 1 Day)

Duration: 1 day, 7 hours 15 minutes elapsed, including 6 hours of instructional time, two short breaks, and a 45-minute lunch
Audience: Professionals who use Gmail, Google Drive, Google Docs, Google Sheets, YouTube, and Gemini in their daily work
Track: Practical generative artificial intelligence for Google Workspace users
Prerequisites: A Google account with access to Gemini and Google Workspace; access to a project email thread and related document for the first lab; a draft or work sample for the fifth lab

## Course Overview

Participants progress from connected data retrieval to structured prompting, document analysis, multimodal work, reusable assistants, and in-app automation. Each session introduces one practical workflow, demonstrates a tested prompt structure, and gives participants time to produce an artifact they can use after class.

## Main Topics Covered

- Connected Workspace retrieval with Gmail, Google Docs, and Google Drive
- Cognitive frameworks for multi-perspective analysis and decision support
- Deep synthesis of long PDF reports and export of cited findings to Google Sheets
- Multimodal analysis of YouTube video, screenshots, user interfaces, and flowcharts
- Persistent personas with Gems and collaborative editing in Gemini Canvas
- Native automation in Google Sheets and Google Docs

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of the course, participants will be able to:

- Retrieve and cross-reference information from Gmail and Google Drive in a single Gemini prompt.
- Use a multi-persona prompt to examine a decision from several viewpoints and verify factual claims.
- Extract, cite, and organize statistical claims from a long PDF report.
- Analyze video arguments and visual interfaces with timestamped and step-by-step prompts.
- Create a reusable Gem and refine a piece of writing in Gemini Canvas.
- Generate a formula-driven project tracker and a related announcement inside Google Workspace.

## Project Context / Tools

The course uses the Gemini web application, Gmail, Google Docs, Google Drive, Google Sheets, Gemini Gems, Gemini Canvas, YouTube, and PDF or image uploads. Participants should use non-sensitive material or prepared sample files during the labs. The instructor provides a dense report, or participants bring an appropriate report, for the data extraction exercise. Workspace feature names and availability can vary by account, so the instructor should demonstrate the current interface before each in-app lab.

## Day 1: From Connected Data to Workspace Automation

### 1. Connected Workspace and Data Retrieval

Introduce the course goals and the shift from manually searching across applications to asking Gemini to retrieve and compare relevant information. Demonstrate a prompt that uses `@Gmail` to find the latest project thread, identify blockers, and compare them with a project specification in Google Docs or Google Drive. Explain how to name the project, constrain the requested output, and ask Gemini to distinguish retrieved evidence from inference.

Launch Breakout Lab 1.1: The Inbox Interrogation. Participants open Gemini, search for a real project or prepared sample sender with `@Gmail`, and cross-reference the result with a specific document or Drive file using `@Google Drive`. They produce a concise status report based on internal data. Remind participants to avoid confidential material when their account or classroom policy does not permit it.

### 2. Cognitive Frameworks

Explain how role assignments, competing viewpoints, explicit output formats, and verification steps can make a prompt easier to evaluate. Demonstrate the board-of-three-experts structure with a cynical risk manager, a visionary optimist, and a pragmatic project manager. Show how to request a debate transcript followed by a consensus table, then use Modify or the double-check feature to inspect factual claims.

Launch Breakout Lab 1.2: The Board of Directors. Participants select a decision such as adopting a software tool, changing a team process, or making a large purchase. They run the multi-persona prompt, check factual claims, and revise the prompt when the roles produce generic or unsupported advice. They finish with a table of risks, rewards, and a recommendation.

### 3. Morning Break

Participants take a short break before the document synthesis session.

### 4. Agentic Workflows and Deep Synthesis

Define an agentic workflow as a sequence in which Gemini handles a substantial task through multiple operations, such as reading a document, extracting evidence, organizing the findings, and critiquing the method. Demonstrate a prompt for extracting every statistical claim from a dense report, including the claim, page number, surrounding context, and methodology critique. Explain why citations, page references, and an explicit instruction not to summarize improve the result.

Launch Breakout Lab 1.3: The Data Miner. Participants upload a large report, run the extraction prompt, inspect the page references and surrounding context, correct weak results, and use Export to Sheets to create a working table of cited findings.

### 5. Lunch

Participants take the scheduled lunch break.

### 6. Multimodal Analysis

Show how Gemini can work with sources beyond plain text. Demonstrate a YouTube prompt that requests three controversial or counter-intuitive points, exact timestamps, and a credible detractor's response. Then demonstrate image analysis with a screenshot of a complex software interface or a messy flowchart. Ask Gemini to map the user flow step by step and identify two areas of friction. Discuss how learners should verify timestamps, interpretations, and claims against the original media.

Launch Breakout Lab 1.4: The X-Ray Vision Test. Participants choose a long YouTube lecture or tutorial and produce timestamped notes focused on specific arguments. They then upload a screenshot and create a visual critique that describes the flow and identifies two friction points.

### 7. Afternoon Break

Participants take a short break before creating a reusable assistant.

### 8. Persistent Personas

Introduce Gems as reusable assistants with saved instructions, a defined role, and a consistent working style. Demonstrate the Relentless Editor instructions, which ask the assistant to identify clichés, expose logical gaps, and ask questions instead of rewriting the text. Move a draft into Gemini Canvas and show how a participant can review, edit, and rewrite selected paragraphs beside the AI output.

Launch Breakout Lab 1.5: Building the Coach. Participants create a Gem for an editing, code review, client communication, or other recurring task. They test it with their own draft, adjust the instructions based on the output, and refine selected paragraphs in Canvas.

### 9. Native Workspace In-App Automation

Connect the day's prompting patterns to work performed inside Google Workspace. Demonstrate how the Help Me Organize sidebar in Google Sheets can create a project tracker with task, owner, status, dropdown values for Pending, In Progress, and Blocked, and a formula that calculates days until a deadline. Then use Help Me Write in Google Docs to draft an announcement based on the project information. Review the generated structure, formulas, and wording before treating the artifacts as ready for use.

Launch Breakout Lab 1.6: The Workspace Sorcerer. Participants create a formula-driven software launch tracker in a blank Google Sheet, verify its dropdowns and deadline calculation, then draft a related announcement in a blank Google Doc. End with a short reflection in which participants identify one workflow they will adapt after the course.

Day 1 Deliverable: A set of practical course outputs, including a project status report, a decision table, a cited data sheet, timestamped video notes, a visual UI critique, a saved Gem with a polished draft, and a Workspace project tracker with a related announcement.

- Lab 1.3: A Google Sheet containing extracted statistical claims with page references and context.

## Teaching Philosophy

The instructor keeps each demonstration short, makes the prompt structure visible, and gives participants time to inspect the output rather than accept it automatically. Every lab ends with a usable artifact and a brief discussion of verification, privacy, and ways to adapt the workflow to daily work.
